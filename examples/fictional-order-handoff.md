# Fictional example: ecommerce order handoff

> Entirely fictional scenario and figures for educational illustration. Not an actual client project or measured outcome.

A six-person ecommerce team handles roughly 80 wholesale orders weekly. Customer service copies approved details from email into a spreadsheet, then warehouse staff re-enter them into a fulfillment tool. Some orders lack delivery instructions.

## Define

Problem: incomplete instructions occasionally reach the warehouse. Owner: operations lead. Objective: reduce exceptions without increasing processing time excessively.

## Measure

In this invented case, a sample of 40 orders has 6 incomplete instructions, an illustrative 15% baseline. The small sample may not represent the full order population.

## Analyze

Two manual re-entry steps and no mandatory delivery-instructions field appear in the workflow. That is a **hypothesis** about the cause, not proven causation.

## Prioritize

Compare: (A) approval checklist, (B) standardized intake form, (C) custom integration. Trial A first because it is low-cost and reversible.

## Improve

Pilot the checklist for 20 orders. Record missing instructions and extra handling time.

## Control

If outcomes improve without unacceptable delays, document the new handoff and review four weeks later. Otherwise, investigate the hypothesis further.

**Lesson:** Clearer processes can sometimes help more than AI or complex automation. No real-world savings or results are claimed.
